Engineering interpretation

Preliminary

Method

Why this is more than a pitch-speed rule

Primary calculation: Wageningen B-series open-water regression. The calculator searches diameter and pitch/diameter ratio for a propeller that absorbs the entered shaft power at the calculated shaft RPM and advance speed, then favours the highest open-water efficiency inside the B-series validity envelope. It also applies the published Reynolds-number correction above Re = 2×10⁶ using an estimated representative blade chord, so the correction remains an approximation rather than a substitute for detailed propeller geometry.

Vessel data: LWL, beam, loaded draft and displacement are used to calculate block coefficient and Froude number. In Auto wake mode the app uses the early-design Taylor wake estimate from block coefficient; this is a guide only and measured/modelled wake is preferable. If known total hull resistance at the design speed is entered, the optimiser also compares propeller thrust against required thrust after the entered thrust-deduction factor.

Electric propulsion: hydrodynamic matching is still based on shaft power, RPM and speed. The electric option emphasises continuous mechanical power and shows a power-limited static-thrust estimate; low shaft RPM and the largest practical diameter generally improve efficiency, subject to aperture, clearance and cavitation constraints.

Predicted speed: the app estimates wetted hull area from displacement and main dimensions (Mumford-type formula), calculates friction with the ITTC-1957 correlation line, and adds a clearly labelled approximate residuary/wave component. It calibrates to known target-speed resistance or a measured trial (if entered). The predicted speed is the calm-water crossing between net propeller thrust and hull resistance, solved with an assumed constant rated shaft-torque envelope up to rated RPM. The uncalibrated wave model is a screening heuristic, not a validated Holtrop–Mennen calculation and not an accuracy guarantee. Full-scale trials/CFD or a full set of hull offsets are required for higher confidence.

References: ITTC resistance-test guidance; Mumford wetted-area approximation. The added wave-resistance curve is expressly a preliminary assumption, not an ITTC or Holtrop formula.

Limits: this is a preliminary fixed-pitch, non-ducted propeller selection tool. Final selection should be checked against actual hull resistance, propeller aperture/tip clearance, blade area/cavitation, gearbox/motor torque limits, shafting, class/flag requirements and propeller manufacturer data.
