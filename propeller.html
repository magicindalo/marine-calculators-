Method

Why this is more than a pitch-speed rule

Primary calculation: Wageningen B-series open-water regression. The calculator searches diameter and pitch/diameter ratio for a propeller that absorbs the entered shaft power at the calculated shaft RPM and advance speed, then favours the highest open-water efficiency inside the B-series validity envelope. It also applies the published Reynolds-number correction above Re = 2×10⁶ using an estimated representative blade chord, so the correction remains an approximation rather than a substitute for detailed propeller geometry.

Wake: propeller advance speed is lower than vessel speed on many single-screw hulls. The presets are only starting assumptions; measured/modelled wake is better. A typical mean wake fraction for single-screw vessels can be around 0.2–0.3, and depends strongly on hull form and propeller position.

Electric propulsion: hydrodynamic matching is still based on shaft power, RPM and speed. The electric option emphasises continuous mechanical power and shows a power-limited static-thrust estimate; low shaft RPM and the largest practical diameter generally improve efficiency, subject to aperture, clearance and cavitation constraints.

Limits: this is a preliminary fixed-pitch, non-ducted propeller selection tool. Final selection should be checked against actual hull resistance, propeller aperture/tip clearance, blade area/cavitation, gearbox/motor torque limits, shafting, class/flag requirements and propeller manufacturer data.
